# Spec: Navigation principles

## Status

Draft

## Purpose

Define the principles that should guide navigation decisions for the unified Health Store and Global Store experience.

## Current evidence

The Phase 1 open card sort suggests that users organise content more strongly by subject, specialty, task, and format than by persona alone.

The in-site search analysis also suggests multiple intent patterns across Health Store and Global Store users, including subject/specialty intent, academic/STEM intent, exam/task intent, product or format intent, commercial intent, and role/persona intent.

## Principles

### 1. Subject and specialty first

Primary navigation should support users browsing by what they need, not only by who they are.

### 2. Task pathways should be explicit

Exam prep, certification, student learning, clinical skills, diagnostics, and procedures should be findable.

### 3. Content format should be visible

Books, eBooks, journals, handbooks, reference content, and digital subscriptions should be supported as content-type routes or filters.

### 4. Persona routes are secondary

Students, nurses, clinicians, researchers, educators, and allied health professionals should have useful pathways, but persona should not be the only primary organising model unless later validation supports it.

### 5. Merchandising is a store layer

Bestsellers, new releases, featured collections, and seasonal shops should support discovery without replacing the main IA.

## Acceptance criteria

A proposed navigation change is ready to discuss when it has:

- a clear problem statement
- evidence from research, analytics, or stakeholder input
- proposed labels or structure
- known risks or boundary cases
- a validation or measurement plan
